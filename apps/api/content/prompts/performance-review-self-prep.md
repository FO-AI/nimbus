    I am preparing for my own performance review covering [PERIOD].

    Summarize my key achievements over that period by reviewing my own email
    and messages. Also pull out any positive feedback I received from
    colleagues or people I worked with, quoting it and naming the source.

    Group the achievements by theme rather than chronologically. Where

## When to use this

Before writing a self-assessment, when six months of work has genuinely
blurred together.

## How to adapt it

- Needs the [licensed Copilot](/guides/microsoft-365-copilot) — it reads
  your own mailbox and calendar.
- Adapted from the UK Government AI Knowledge Hub, with British public-
  sector terms replaced by UNC equivalents. The original is written for a UK
  civil service mid-year review; the structure fits a Carolina Talent self-
  assessment unchanged.
- Add your actual goals for the period so the themes match what you will be
  assessed against.
- Run it a month before the deadline. The underdocumented items are worth a
  conversation with your supervisor before the review, not during it.
- New to prompting, or not getting what you need?
  [How to write a prompt](/guides/how-to-write-a-prompt) covers the CLEAR
  framework and the Microsoft Prompt Gallery.

## What to check

- **This is for reviewing your own work only.** Using AI to evaluate, rate,
  or assess another employee is prohibited by the Staff Generative AI Usage
  Guidance — see
  [what data can I put into an AI tool](/guides/sensitive-data). Do not run
  this against anyone else's mailbox, and do not use its output to write
  about someone you supervise.
- It summarizes what you wrote about, which correlates weakly with what
  mattered. The gaps it flags are the useful part.
- Quoted feedback should be checked against the original message before you
  repeat it in a review.
