## What it is

Before software handles University data, the Information Security Office
assesses it: what data it touches, where that data goes, how the vendor
secures it, and what the contract says happens if something goes wrong. It is
not an approval rubber stamp and it is not only for large purchases.

## When F&O runs into it

- A unit wants an AI tool that is not in [the registry](/guides)
- A vendor turns on an AI feature in a system you already license — this still
  counts as new processing, even though nothing was purchased
- A pilot that started as free is about to handle real data
- A contract renewal changes where data is stored or who can access it

## What the review asks for

Have these ready and it moves faster:

- What business problem it solves, and what already-approved tool does not
- The data it will touch, by [tier](/guides/information-classification-standard)
- Whether the vendor trains models on customer data, and whether that can be
  turned off in the contract rather than in a settings page
- Who at UNC owns it after go-live

## The order that matters

The review comes **before** the purchase. A signed contract does not make an
assessment a formality — it makes a failed assessment expensive. The
[Staff Generative AI Usage Guidance](/guides/staff-ai-usage-guidance) is
explicit that you consult your Senior IT contact before adopting or buying a
GenAI product.

The full path, with who does what, is in
[getting an AI tool approved for your unit](/guides/getting-a-tool-approved).
