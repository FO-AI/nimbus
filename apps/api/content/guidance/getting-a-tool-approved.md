## When you need this page

- Your unit wants an AI tool that is not in [the registry](/guides)
- A vendor has switched on an AI feature in a system you already use
- Somebody has started a free trial with real University data in it
- You want a [Copilot Studio agent](/guides/copilot-studio) over your unit's
  documents

The last one has its own front door (the AI Acceleration Program) but still
lands on this path if it touches data above Tier 1.

## Step 0 — check whether you need a new tool at all

Most F&O requests turn out to be something an approved tool already does.
Before starting a review, look at what is already available: Copilot Chat is
free with an Onyen, the licensed tier works inside Excel, Word, and Outlook,
and [PromptLab](/guides/promptlab) gives you several models at no cost. A
review takes weeks; checking takes an afternoon.

## The path

**1. Your unit's Senior IT contact.** Always first. The
[Staff Generative AI Usage Guidance](/guides/staff-ai-usage-guidance) says to
consult them before adopting *or buying* a GenAI product, and they will know
whether another unit already asked.

**2. University Data Assistance form.** This starts the record. It asks what
the tool does, what data it touches, and who owns it.

**3. Information Security Office review.** ISO assesses the vendor and the
data flow — where data goes, how it is secured, whether the vendor trains on
it, what the contract says. See
[software and vendor risk assessment](/guides/software-vendor-risk-assessment).

**4. Data Governance Oversight Group.** Required where sensitive data is
involved. DGOG approval is what makes a Tier 2 use legitimate; no individual
can authorize it on their own.

**5. Procurement.** Once it is approved, the purchase follows the normal path.

## What makes a request move quickly

- **A named business problem**, not a tool looking for a use
- **Data identified by [tier](/guides/information-classification-standard)** —
  guessing here is the most common cause of a second round of questions
- **An answer on model training** taken from the contract, not the marketing page
- **A named owner** who will still be responsible after go-live
- **A reason the approved tools do not work**, since that is the first question

## While you wait

Nothing above Tier 0 goes into the tool. That is the whole rule, and the one
most often broken by a pilot that "was only a test." A trial with real
University data in it is not a trial, it is unapproved processing.

If you need to demonstrate value in the meantime, use public material — the
same [practice documents](/guides/practice-budget-book-variance) the playbooks
use are Tier 0 and published.

## Do not buy first

A signed contract does not shorten the review; it just means a failed review
is expensive. This is also true for an AI feature bundled into a renewal — new
processing is new processing, even when the invoice does not change.
