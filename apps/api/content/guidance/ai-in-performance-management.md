## Who this is for

Anyone in Finance & Operations who supervises staff and is wondering whether
Copilot can help with appraisal season. The short answer is: a little, at the
edges, and never for the judgment itself.

## What is allowed

AI may help you **express** an assessment you have already made:

- Tightening feedback you drafted yourself into clearer language
- Restructuring a long set of notes into the appraisal form's sections
- Suggesting neutral phrasing for something you wrote bluntly

The judgment has to exist before the tool touches it.

## What is not

- **Producing a rating or a score.** Not a draft rating, not a suggested one.
- **Screening or ranking candidates**, or drafting interview evaluations.
- **Generating the record behind a disciplinary action.**
- **Summarizing an employee's activity** — their email, their Teams messages,
  their calendar — to build a picture of how they are performing.
- **Putting employee data into a tool that is not University-provisioned.**
  Personnel information is Tier 2; a consumer chatbot is not an option.

The [Staff Generative AI Usage Guidance](/guides/staff-ai-usage-guidance)
prohibits using AI to hire, evaluate, or discipline employees. OHR's page is
the supervisor-facing version of that same line.

## Why the prompt library looks the way it does

This is the reason Nimbus has no interview-question prompts, no candidate
summary prompts, and no performance-feedback prompts, even though the external
libraries Nimbus imported from are full of them. The ones that survived
screening describe **work** rather than people: a
[position description](/prompts/position-description-draft), an
[onboarding checklist](/prompts/new-hire-setup-checklist), a
[training outline](/prompts/training-session-outline).

The one prompt in the library about a review —
[preparing for your own review](/prompts/performance-review-self-prep) — is
written for you to run against your own record, and says so.
