## Why this is an F&O page

Most AI guidance is about what you may put into a tool. This one is about what
somebody else is doing with the same technology, and it lands hardest on the
staff who change bank details, release payments, and process reimbursements.

A convincing voice clone needs a few seconds of audio — a recorded webinar, a
public meeting, a voicemail greeting. A video call with a familiar face is no
longer evidence of who you are talking to.

## The shape of the attack

It is a routine request with urgency attached:

- A vendor emails that their remittance details have changed, with an invoice
  attached that matches your real PO
- A voice you recognize calls asking you to push a wire through before close
- A short video call from a leader's account authorizes an exception
- A new employee asks Payroll to redirect direct deposit before their first run

None of these are unusual on their own. That is the point.

## Controls that still work

**Verify on a channel you already had.** Call back on the number in
ConnectCarolina or on the contract — never a number in the message that
prompted the call. This is the single control that survives a good deepfake.

**Treat banking changes as their own process.** A change of remittance
details is a separate, verified transaction, not an edit to an invoice. Same
for direct deposit changes.

**Let urgency raise suspicion rather than lower it.** Pressure to skip a step
is the tell. No genuine University payment fails because it was verified.

**Keep dual control on the path that moves money**, even when the requester is
senior. Seniority is what is being impersonated.

## If a request does not verify

Stop the payment and report it — ITS Help Portal or 919-962-HELP (4357), and
your unit's finance lead. A held payment costs a day; a released one is
usually gone.

## Worth saying to your team

AI did not create payment fraud, it removed the errors that used to give it
away — the odd phrasing, the wrong accent, the crude forgery. Anything your
process relied on *looking* or *sounding* right needs replacing with a step
that checks.
