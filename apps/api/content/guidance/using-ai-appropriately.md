## The short version

The Provost's AI Committee published a single page of do and don't. It is the
thing to hand a colleague who is about to use Copilot for the first time and
does not want to read a standard.

## Four points

**Use it openly.** AI use is not something to hide. If it helped produce work
that goes to someone else, you should be comfortable saying so — and where the
work carries ethical or legal weight, [you have to](/guides/staff-ai-usage-guidance).

**Verify the output.** Generative tools produce confident text regardless of
whether it is right. Figures, citations, policy references, and names all get
checked against the system of record before the work leaves your hands.

**Keep confidential material out.** Approved tools handle Tier 1 and 2; never
Tier 3. Consumer chatbots handle none of it — see
[AI, data privacy and you](/guides/ai-data-privacy).

**You own the result.** The tool is not accountable for what goes out under
your name. Authorship, accuracy, and consequences stay with you.
